    \subsection{Numerical Results}
    Shale's Round-Robin structure provides a predictable and stable performance across different traffic patterns. Using the waterfilling algorithm, we observe that Shale effectively utilizes available pathways, though it may not reach the peak efficiency of more dynamic architectures under extreme skew.

    \subsubsection{Advanced Congestion Control and VLB Routing}
    To handle real-world traffic demands, our implementation of Shale incorporates:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Interleaved Schedules}: Simulation of parallel matching schedules to support both low-latency ($h=1$) and high-throughput ($h=4$) traffic streams.
        \item \textbf{Valiant Load Balancing (VLB)}: A two-phase routing scheme where cells are first sprayed to intermediate nodes (Spraying Phase) before being routed directly to the destination (Direct Phase). This spreads the load and reduces hotspots.
        \item \textbf{PIEO Queues}: Push-In Extract-Out queues allow switches to extract the highest-priority eligible cell, overcoming Head-Of-Line (HOL) blocking when the physical link for the top cell is not currently connected in the timeslot.
        \item \textbf{Credit-Based Flow Control}: Tokens are exchanged between switches to manage buffer space (credits), preventing bufferbloat and ensuring hop-by-hop reliability.
    \end{itemize}

    \section{Genetic Algorithm}
    To go beyond fixed architectures, we developed a hybrid Genetic Algorithm (GA). The goal is to evolve a topology $W$ that maximizes fitness:
    \[
        Fitness = \alpha \cdot Capacity(W, T) + \beta \cdot \frac{1}{ASPL(W)} - \gamma \cdot Penalty(Degree)
    \]
    where $T$ is the traffic demand (e.g., skewed or hotspot traffic). We often initialize the population with a "Frozen Ring Backbone" to ensure basic connectivity (cycle graph) and let the GA evolve long-range shortcuts.

    \subsection{Numerical Results}
    The GA-evolved topologies were tested against stochastic waterfilling to ensure robustness. The results show that the GA successfully identifies long-range shortcuts that minimize the average shortest path length (ASPL) while maximizing Shannon capacity for the target traffic.

    \section{Performance Comparison}
    We benchmarked all architectures—Opera, Shale, Sirius, and the Genetic Algorithm—under a skewed traffic pattern. The results, summarized in Figure \ref{fig:final_comp}, demonstrate the clear advantage of topology evolution. While static architectures like Opera and Shale perform well under uniform assumptions, they struggle with highly non-uniform demands where specific node pairs dominate the traffic.

    The Genetic Algorithm, by evolving its connectivity specifically for the skewed traffic matrix, effectively "pre-allocates" capacity where it is most needed, resulting in significantly higher total Shannon capacity for the same aggregate power budget.
